End-to-End Pipeline

How the System Works

Visual information flows through feature abstraction, linear hidden projection, and autoregressive sequential word decoding.

Stage 01
Image Preprocessing

Input image is converted to RGB, resized to 256px, center-cropped to 224×224, and normalized with ImageNet statistics.

Stage 02
ResNet-50 Encoder

Frozen convolutional backbone extracts a compact 2048-dimensional visual feature vector representing semantic image content.

Stage 03
Linear Hidden State Init

A linear projection with tanh activation transforms the 2048-d features into the initial hidden states h₀ for both GRU layers.

Stage 04
2-Layer GRU Decoder

At each timestep t, word embeddings (256-d) and previous hidden states predict the probability distribution over 2,656 vocabulary words.

Stage 05
Beam Search Generation

Maintains the top k candidate hypotheses at each step until <end> token is emitted, producing a grammatical sentence.

Comparative Design

Two models. One image.

We compare a massive pre-trained multimodal vision-language foundation model against our compact convolutional recurrent model trained from scratch.

Baseline Foundation Model

Salesforce BLIP (Base)

A pretrained vision-language foundation model pre-trained on millions of image-text pairs with CapFilt bootstrap filtering and fine-tuned on COCO. Used as a strong reference baseline.

  • Encoder ArchitectureVision Transformer (ViT-B/16)
  • Language DecoderMultimodal Transformer (12 layers)
  • Pre-training Data14M Web pairs + COCO fine-tuning
  • Parameter Scale~224 Million parameters
  • Local RoleReference & Pseudo-ground-truth
Our Custom Architecture

CNN + 2-Layer GRU

Our custom architecture trained from scratch on Flickr8k. Uses a frozen ResNet-50 visual encoder to eliminate GPU compute bottleneck, paired with a custom 2-layer recurrent GRU language decoder.

  • Encoder ArchitectureResNet-50 (Frozen 2048-d)
  • Language Decoder2-Layer GRU (512 hidden units)
  • Training DatasetFlickr8k (6,473 images / 32k pairs)
  • Vocabulary Size2,656 tokens (min-freq ≥ 5)
  • RegularizationDropout 0.5 + Early Stopping

Academic Transparency

Where Caption Lens struggles

Caption generation quality degrades as scene complexity increases. Real generative systems encounter distinct failure modes due to architecture and dataset boundaries.

Subject Competition
Multiple Objects & Crowds

When an image contains multiple people, vehicles, and overlapping elements, the model must choose which object to prioritize. Simpler single-subject images succeed reliably, whereas crowded scenes often cause omission of key background subjects.

Feature Dissipation
Small & Occluded Objects

Objects that are distant, partially obscured, or low in pixel density produce weak activation footprints in the 2048-d ResNet feature map. The decoder frequently produces overly generic descriptions instead of recognizing subtle props.

Relational Bottleneck
Spatial & Action Relationships

Detecting individual object classes is substantially easier than understanding spatial interactions (e.g., who is holding what or standing behind whom). A non-attentive GRU decoder cannot selectively attend to spatial coordinates across timesteps.

Vocabulary Bounds
Dataset Coverage Limits

Flickr8k contains 8,000 images focused on outdoor recreation and animals. Unfamiliar domains (scientific instruments, anime art, medical scans) are mapped to familiar approximations (e.g., a snowmobile predicted as a "bike in the snow").

Error Propagation
Sequential Decoding Cascade

Because the GRU generates captions autoregressively, predicting word wₜ conditioned on wₜ₋₁, an incorrect early token steers subsequent hidden states away from ground truth, occasionally resulting in repetitive phrasing.

Generative Plausibility
Caption Hallucination

The language model may generate a plausible-sounding grammatical phrase (such as "in a grassy park") simply because that n-gram appeared frequently in training annotations alongside "a dog", even if the image background is concrete.
