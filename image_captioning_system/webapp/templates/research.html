Comparative Analysis: Multimodal Transformer vs. Convolutional Recurrent Captioning

An empirical study evaluating zero-shot multimodal vision-language pretraining against an end-to-end custom ResNet-50 + 2-layer GRU architecture trained under strict data-split constraints on Flickr8k.

Authors: DS Lab Research Group
Dataset: Flickr8k (80/10/10 by-image split)
Hardware: NVIDIA RTX 4050 (6GB VRAM)
Framework: PyTorch • CUDA
Section 01

Mathematical & Architectural Formulation

Understanding the end-to-end visual-to-linguistic transformation pipeline.

Visual Feature Bottleneck

Given an input RGB image \( I \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 224 \times 224} \), we discard the final classification layer of a pre-trained ResNet-50 network and extract the global average pooled bottleneck vector:

f(I) = \text{ResNet50}_{\text{frozen}}(I) \in \mathbb{R}^{2048}

Keeping the convolutional weights frozen ensures fast, reproducible training without gradient drift or memory exhaustion on local workstation GPUs.

Hidden State Initialization

Rather than feeding the visual vector at every decoding step, our model injects the visual context exclusively into the initial hidden states of the stacked 2-layer GRU:

h_0^{(1..2)} = \tanh\left( W_{\text{init}} f(I) + b_{\text{init}} \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times B \times 512}

This initializes the internal recurrent memory with the entire visual scene before token sequence generation begins.

Autoregressive Language Generation & Search Decoding

During training, teacher forcing feeds ground-truth tokens \( w_t \) through an embedding matrix \( E \in \mathbb{R}^{2656 \times 256} \). The GRU updates its recurrent memory via gating:

  • Update Gate: \( z_t = \sigma(W_z x_t + U_z h_{t-1} + b_z) \)
  • Reset Gate: \( r_t = \sigma(W_r x_t + U_r h_{t-1} + b_r) \)
  • Candidate State: \( \tilde{h}_t = \tanh(W_h x_t + U_h (r_t \odot h_{t-1}) + b_h) \)
  • Hidden Update: \( h_t = (1 - z_t) \odot h_{t-1} + z_t \odot \tilde{h}_t \)

A dense linear projection computes unnormalized logits over vocabulary \( V \):

P(w_{t+1} \mid w_{\le t}) = \text{softmax}(W_{\text{vocab}} h_t^{(2)} + b_{\text{vocab}})

At inference, Beam Search (width \( k = 3 \)) keeps the top candidate sequences with length normalization \( \frac{1}{|Y|^\alpha} \sum \log P(w_t) \), significantly outperforming greedy argmax decoding.

Section 05

Taxonomy of Model Failure Modes

Detailed qualitative categorization of common failure vectors observed during test evaluations.

CASE TYPE: CONCEPT SUBSTITUTION

Out-of-Vocabulary Semantic Mapping

When presented with objects outside the Flickr8k 2,656-word dictionary (e.g., snowmobiles or skateboards), the recurrent decoder activates the nearest dense visual cluster. In sample 109738916_236dc456ac.jpg, where people rode snowmobiles, our model outputted:

"a man in a red jacket is riding a bike in the snow"

The model correctly identified the environment (snow) and action (riding), but substituted "snowmobile" with "bike".

CASE TYPE: REPETITION LOOP

Autoregressive Language Loops

Even powerful foundation models like BLIP suffer from degeneration when beam scores collapse on ambiguous textures. In our test photo 4_food_berries.jpg, BLIP generated:

"fresh straw straw straw straw straw straw straw straw straw straw..."

This demonstrates that without explicit repetition penalties, language decoders can become trapped in cyclic n-gram loops.
